As a result of the implementation of the proposed methods in this thesis, there are many unexplored methods that are now available to use. Specifically, the biased intervals have debiased counterparts where the normal likelihoods are not re-penalized. While the crux of this thesis was arguing for biased intervals, I am not naive in thinking they will become the norm, so their debiased alternatives should be considered for their performances relative to other debiased methods. The idea of debiased PIPE CIs is not new, but one that was first proposed by \cite{dai2019} as part of potential future work. However, I have carried out implementation of these intervals along with the methods I have proposed. That the work of \cite{dai2019} on false discovery rate control with PIPE potentially now has the Local Quadratic Approximation alternative to consider. This is particularly of interest as \cite{dai2019} found that PIPE was generally conservative and LQA was in large part designed to remedy this issue.

Another idea worth exploring is constructing the Laplace-Normal distributions under the null, $\beta_j = 0$, and then computing the p-value for the observed lasso estimate. For example, if such p-values could be reliably used for FDR control, then this would provide an alternative route that clearly relates back to the lasso point estimates.

My satisfaction would be a future where biased intervals are more common place and are the default for reporting in practice. While they do not provide frequentist coverage properties, they provide correct average coverage and communicate the uncertainty in the estimates. This ultimately provide insights that are undiscernible from the estimates alone, something that can not be clearly state for debiased procedures. With that said, further work is needed to continue to define what properties biased intervals should have and how to evaluate them in a wider scope.

While not a rigorous justification, there is a reasons why this was not a primary concern. Specifically, we found that the Reid estimator tends to over estimate the error variance and as such the intervals are generally over conservative for average coverage. Nevertheless, it is something that should not be excluded from future exploration.

In this dissertation I argued and proposed methods for intervals that remain consistent with the assumptions that were made when selecting a sparse penalty. Not only this, but I showed preliminary evidence that biased intervals may provide better coverage behaviors for selected variables. Additionally, I proposed three viable methods as well as how these methods can be extended to other penalties (SCAD, MCP, -NET) and other outcome types (poisson, bernoulli). With these extensions in place, I furthered the argument for biased intervals using the bias reducing penalties SCAD and MCP and constructing intervals around their estimates using the methods proposed which even further provided coverage benefits over de-biased lasso based approaches. All the methods described in this thesis have been implemented in the \texttt{confidence\_intervals} function in the \texttt{ncvreg} package. To my knowledge, this is the widest coverage of confidence interval construction for penalized regression approaches.

In the end, it is my hope that if the lasso were a being that it would be reading this thesis with a smile.

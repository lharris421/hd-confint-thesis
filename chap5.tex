While not a rigorous justification, there is a reasons why this was not a primary concern. Specifically, we found that the Reid estimator tends to over estimate the error variance and as such the intervals are generally over conservative for average coverage. Nevertheless, it is something that should not be excluded from future exploration.

Further work is needed to continue to define what properties average coverage intervals should have and how to evaluate them in a wider scope. In particular, it is unclear in general what the correct coverage rate is for a single parameter under a given penalty. Being able to determine this more broadly would allow the assessment of which method has better performance in setups such as Figure~\ref{fig:3-man2}. Along these same lines, it would be helpful to attach a confidence level with individual intervals to say, for example, ``we expect $\beta_3$ to be covered 99\% of the time and $\beta_7$, 40\% of the time.'' 

Another avenue worth exploring is constructing the Laplace-Normal distributions under the null, $\beta_j = 0$, and then computing the p-value for the observed estimate. If such p-values could be reliably used for FDR control, then this would provide an alternative approach that directly relates back to the lasso point estimates. Furthermore, as a result of the implementation of the proposed methods in this thesis, there are many unexplored methods that are now available to use. Specifically, the biased intervals have debiased counterparts where the normal likelihoods are not re-penalized. While the crux of this thesis was arguing for biased intervals, I am not naive in thinking they will become the norm, so their debiased alternatives should be considered for their performances relative to other debiased methods. The idea of debiased PIPE CIs is not new, but one that was first proposed by \cite{dai2019} as part of potential future work. However, I have carried out implementation of these intervals along with the methods I have proposed. Additionally, the work of \cite{dai2019} on false discovery rate control with PIPE now has the Local Quadratic Approximation alternative to consider. This is particularly of interest as \cite{dai2019} found that PIPE was generally conservative; the LQA based approach was suggested in this thesis to help remedy this issue.

My satisfaction would be a future where biased intervals are the default for reporting in practice alongside biased estimation methods. While they do not provide traditional frequentist coverage properties, they provide correct average coverage and communicate the uncertainty in the estimates. This ultimately provide insights that are undiscernible from the estimates alone, something that cannot be stated for debiased procedures.

In this dissertation I argued for intervals that remain consistent with the assumptions that were made when selecting a sparse penalty. Not only this, but I showed preliminary evidence that biased intervals may provide better coverage behaviors for selected variables. Additionally, I proposed three viable methods as well as how these methods can be extended to other penalties (SCAD, MCP, -Net) and other outcome types (poisson, bernoulli). With these extensions in place, I furthered the argument for biased intervals using the bias reducing penalties, SCAD and MCP. Constructing intervals around their estimates using the methods proposed further provided coverage benefits over de-biased lasso based approaches. All the methods described in this thesis have been implemented in the \texttt{confidence\_intervals} function in the \texttt{ncvreg} package. To my knowledge, this is the widest coverage of confidence interval methods for penalized regression approaches in R.

In the end, it is my hope if the lasso were alive that it would be reading this thesis with a smile, knowing that for once it has its own intervals, not that it was just used on the path to constructing them.
